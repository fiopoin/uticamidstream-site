Midstream: crude quality

Bakken crude backs up as Enbridge's North Dakota line stays down over contamination

A 475,000 barrel a day pipeline into Minnesota has been out for days after tests reportedly found organic chlorides in Bakken crude. One Midwest refiner has reportedly declared force majeure, and shippers are looking at Dakota Access.

Enbridge says routine crude oil quality testing has caused delays on its North Dakota Pipeline, which carries up to 475,000 barrels a day of Bakken crude to Clearbrook, Minnesota. It is working with shippers to return to normal operations and has given no timeline. Argus reported the system has been shut in since November 14; Enbridge has not confirmed a shutdown.

Sources told Reuters that elevated levels of organic chlorides were found in Bakken crude late last week. Organic chlorides can corrode refinery equipment, so refiners test for them closely. An Energy Aspects note said at least one Midwest refiner declared force majeure, and a Reuters source named Flint Hills Resources' Pine Bend refinery in Minnesota. Flint Hills did not respond. Clearbrook also supplies Marathon Petroleum's St. Paul Park refinery.

The outage lands in a soft crude market. WTI settled at $83.26 on Wednesday, its lowest close since August 26, after the EIA reported a 3.4 million barrel crude build to 417.8 million barrels. Energy Aspects expects the lost Bakken supply to tighten Midcontinent light sweet markets even so.

Sources: Reuters via BOE Report; Argus via Seeking Alpha; Construction Review; FinanceFeeds

Along the chain

  1. Upstream

    Bakken producers need an outlet for barrels that would normally move east to Clearbrook. Wellhead contamination, if confirmed, can mean testing and treatment costs that land back on shippers.

  2. Midstream

    Energy Transfer's 750,000 b/d Dakota Access Pipeline to Patoka is the obvious alternative, along with rail. Crude quality control at injection points is now the story to watch.

  3. Downstream

    Pine Bend, about 320,000 b/d, and St. Paul Park, about 105,000 b/d, lean on Clearbrook. Buyers are seeking replacement barrels and avoiding possible contamination.

  4. Market

    Light sweet crude in the Midcontinent should firm against a weak WTI. The signal to watch is Enbridge's restart timing and whether more refiners declare force majeure.

Use it

Producers
Bakken operators should check nominations, storage and alternative routes now, and review how quality specs are tested at their gathering points.
Midstream
Quality failures can shut a line as surely as a leak. Review chloride testing, segregation and tariff quality rules on your crude systems.
Refiners and traders
Upper Midwest light sweet supply is short. Price replacement barrels against Dakota Access and rail costs.
Investors
Outage length is the variable. A short disruption moves regional differentials; a long one becomes a story about liability and volumes.

Across the chain

Upstream

OnshoreUnited States

Diamondback and Chevron put billions into acreage and deepwater barrels

Diamondback Energy agreed on November 16 to buy Double Eagle's remaining Midland basin interest for about $2.15 billion in cash plus about 15.56 million Diamondback shares, at about 4.0 times estimated 2027 EBITDA. The deal adds about 176,000 net acres, more than 1,200 gross horizontal locations and an expected 92,000 boe/d in 2027, and is due to close December 21. Separately, the Financial Times reported Chevron is near a roughly $5 billion deal for a majority stake in Blackstone backed Beacon Offshore Energy, which holds 70 deepwater leases in the Gulf of America.

Why it matters. Buyers are paying for inventory even with WTI near $83. Core Permian locations and deepwater leases are the two kinds of acreage that still compete for capital in a falling price market.

Sources: World Oil; Reuters via Offshore Engineer

Upstream

GlobalGlobal

IEA says the oil market is back in surplus for the first time since the Iran war began

The IEA's November report, released November 13, said global oil inventories rose by 1.7 million barrels a day in October, the first surplus since the war began in late February. Supply has risen by 5.3 million b/d since August. Gulf oil exports outside Oman reached 18.4 million b/d in October, still 26% below prewar levels, and Gulf product and LPG exports are about half of prewar. The agency sees 2026 demand falling 2.7 million b/d to 102.3 million b/d, with a 2 million b/d recovery in 2027.

Why it matters. The war shock has turned into an inventory build. That is the backdrop for the OPEC+ meeting on November 29, and it explains why Hormuz incidents now move prices less than they did.

Sources: The National; OilPrice.com

Regulation

OnshoreUnited States

FERC takes up Sabine Pass expansion and a new Waha pipeline at today's meeting

FERC's November 19 open meeting lists six gas certificate items. They include Cheniere's Sabine Pass expansion with three new liquefaction trains and a 55.6 mile pipeline in Louisiana, the 35.9 mile Forza Pipeline carrying 750,000 Dth/d to the Waha hub in New Mexico and Texas, Mountain Valley's 600,000 Dth/d Boost compression project, a Columbia Gas LNG storage project in Virginia, and Texas Eastern's Line 31 expansion to serve Entergy's Traceview power station in Mississippi.

Why it matters. Listed items may be decided today. Together they cover LNG export growth, Permian gas gathering into Waha and new pipe for power plants, the three demand themes behind this year's midstream buildout.

Sources: White & Case

Upstream

OffshoreGuyana

Uaru starts up and lifts Guyana's capacity to 1.1 million barrels a day

ExxonMobil started production at Uaru, the fifth development on the Stabroek block, on November 16. The MODEC built FPSO Errea Wittu is expected to reach an initial 250,000 b/d in the first quarter of 2027 and can store 2 million barrels. ExxonMobil holds 45%, Hess Guyana Exploration 30% and CNOOC 25%. Whiptail is due in 2027, Hammerhead in 2029, and Longtail, the first gas and condensate project, reached FID this month.

Why it matters. Guyana adds barrels into a market the IEA already sees in surplus. Non OPEC growth like this is part of what OPEC+ has to weigh on November 29.

Sources: World Oil

Leader of the week · Week of November 16, 2026

DA

Dan Ammann

President, ExxonMobil Upstream Company

This month Ammann's upstream business took a $12.5 billion final investment decision on Longtail, a Stabroek development that targets non-associated gas and condensate rather than oil. He has run ExxonMobil's upstream business since February 1, 2025, after leading its Low Carbon Solutions unit from 2022.

Before ExxonMobil he was chief executive of Cruise, the GM majority owned self driving company, from 2018, and president of General Motors from 2014. When he moved to upstream he argued that meeting energy demand is an "and" equation: more supply and lower emissions together.

Why we are watching. An executive from autos and technology now runs one of the most closely followed growth assets in oil. Longtail tests whether Guyana can turn gas into a second engine, and his calls on pace and cost there will shape Stabroek's next decade.

Sources: ExxonMobil investor release; Oil & Gas Journal; Industrial Info
