Market: the war premium fades

Oil falls to a three month low as the Iran war premium fades and stocks of crude build

WTI slipped under $80 early Tuesday after a 4.5% drop on Monday, even as Iran said three more tankers hit mines near Hormuz.

Crude fell for a second day. On Monday, WTI for January lost 4.5% to $80.17 and Brent 3.5% to $89.37, according to Bloomberg data cited by GOLDINVEST, after President Trump told Fox News he would give diplomacy with Iran more time. By 0510 GMT on Tuesday, Reuters reported, Brent was down another 66 cents at $88.71 after its lowest close since August 31, and WTI was at $79.57 after touching its lowest since August 26.

Supply is the other weight. Deutsche Bank said observed global liquids inventories rose 52 million barrels in October, which it said implies oversupply of about 1.7 million barrels a day, Reuters reported. The IEA expects a surplus of about 2 million barrels a day this quarter, GOLDINVEST noted. In Libya, the Sharara pipeline reopened after an armed group closed a valve, the National Oil Corporation's chair told Reuters on Monday.

The shipping risk has not gone away. Iran's Revolutionary Guards navy said three "violating" tankers caught fire after hitting mines in the strait on Monday, according to Press TV, and the Wall Street Journal said the US is investigating whether Iran was behind cyberattacks on as many as 50 tankers. Kpler data show about 9.5 million barrels a day moved through Hormuz in the ten days to November 19, down from 12.2 million in October. "Brent has largely lost any upward impetus in recent days," said Suvro Sarkar of DBS Bank. OPEC+ ministers meet on November 29.

Sources: Reuters via The Express Tribune; GOLDINVEST, citing Bloomberg and Kpler; OilPrice.com

Along the chain

  1. Upstream

    WTI near $80 trims 2027 cash flow plans and makes hedging the strip less attractive than it was a week ago.

  2. Midstream

    Tankers still face mine and cyber risk at Hormuz, so freight and war risk cover east of the strait stay expensive even as crude falls.

  3. Downstream

    Cheaper crude helps refiners, but gasoline and diesel futures fell nearly as fast, and the 3-2-1 crack held near $39 a barrel.

  4. Market

    Traders are pricing surplus over disruption. A Brent to WTI spread near $9 and rising inventories point to a looser market into OPEC+.

Across the chain

Upstream

OnshoreUnited States

TotalEnergies buys more US gas from Continental Resources

TotalEnergies said on November 23 it agreed to acquire interests in US natural gas assets from Continental Resources, adding about 150 MMcf/d of net production, roughly 26,000 boe/d, by 2032. Neither the price nor the basin was disclosed; Oil & Gas Journal said the assets are tied into existing midstream lines. The deal follows TotalEnergies' 2025 purchase of Anadarko basin interests from Continental and its 2024 Eagle Ford deals with EOG Resources and Lewis Energy. With its operated Barnett position, the company expects net US gas output of about 850 MMcf/d from 2032.

Why it matters. TotalEnergies was the largest US LNG exporter in 2025 at about 19 million tonnes, Pipeline & Gas Journal reported. Owning more of its own feedgas hedges that export book against Henry Hub swings.

Sources: Oil & Gas Journal; Pipeline & Gas Journal

Upstream

OffshoreEgypt, Eastern Mediterranean

Egypt presses Chevron and Eni to speed up the Nargis gas field

Egypt's petroleum minister, Karim Badawi, met Chevron and Eni executives on Saturday, November 21, to push for faster development of the offshore Nargis field, Construction Review Online reported. The Nargis-2 well, drilled with the Stena Forth, reached more than 5,000 metres and found up to 35 metres of net gas pay, the ministry said on October 24. Chevron operates the concession with Eni, Mubadala Energy and Egypt's Tharwa Petroleum. No production date or cost for a third well was given; the companies agreed to hold regular technical meetings.

Why it matters. Nargis-1, found in December 2022, was put at around 3.5 Tcf of gas in reports at the time, an estimate. A firm plan would add an East Mediterranean supply source for Egypt and its partners.

Sources: Construction Review Online

Midstream

OnshorePermian basin, Texas and New Mexico

Permian gas flaring heads for a three year low as new pipes take the gas

Flaring in the Permian is on track for its lowest monthly level in three years as added pipeline capacity moves surplus gas to other Texas markets, Bloomberg reported on November 23. Flaring is averaging about 268 MMcf/d this month, the lowest since November 2023, Construction Review Online said, against close to 500 MMcf/d estimated in mid 2025. Energy Transfer's 42 inch Hugh Brinson line from Waha toward the Dallas and Fort Worth area entered full service for Phase I on September 1, with Phase II due in the first quarter of 2027.

Why it matters. Less flaring means more gas sold and fewer days of negative Waha prices, which averaged about minus $2.19 per MMBtu in the first half of 2026.

Sources: Seeking Alpha, citing Bloomberg; Construction Review Online

Gas and LNG

OnshoreUS Lower 48

Henry Hub swings 31 cents as December options expire today

December NYMEX gas settled at $3.411 per MMBtu on Friday, up 4.4%, then fell about 31 cents on Monday morning, Rigzone reported from EBW Analytics. NGI said weekend weather models warmed the early December outlook, while a pre-Thanksgiving cold shot pushed physical prices up across most of the Lower 48, with New England hubs gaining more than $1 and Appalachian hubs back above $2. December options expire on Tuesday and the contract settles on Wednesday.

Why it matters. EBW's Eli Rubin expects volatility to continue into expiry and a structurally weak market over the next 30 to 45 days, a signal for producers deciding whether to curtail in Appalachia.

Sources: Rigzone, citing EBW Analytics; Natural Gas Intelligence

Leader of the week · Week of November 23, 2026

NW

Nick Walker

Chief executive, Vår Energi

Walker's company agreed on November 23 to buy ConocoPhillips' Norway business and its Teesside terminal stake for an enterprise value of $4.425 billion, taking over operatorship of the Greater Ekofisk Area and the Norpipe export system. He has led Vår Energi, Norway's Eni controlled independent, since September 2023.

Before Vår he was chief executive of Lundin Energy until its sale to Aker BP in mid 2022, and he earlier worked at BP, Talisman Energy, Africa Oil and Cairn Oil & Gas under Vedanta. Vår says he has more than 30 years in technical, commercial and executive roles. He studied mining engineering at Imperial College London and computer science at University College London, and holds an MBA from City University Business School.

Why we are watching. The deal, on top of the BlueNord combination, puts Vår among the largest independent offshore producers, with a target of more than 500,000 boe/d into the mid 2030s. How he runs the ageing Ekofisk hub and its UK link will shape a large share of Norway's southern North Sea output.

Sources: Vår Energi executive committee; Offshore Engineer; ConocoPhillips release; Offshore Energy
