Market: Hormuz and the blockade

A tanker incident at the US blockade line tests oil's calm as Iran stages tankers at Kharg

UKMTO reported a tanker incident involving military forces in the Gulf of Oman on Sunday. Futures barely moved early Monday, with Brent near $92.

UK Maritime Trade Operations said on Sunday, November 22, that a tanker was involved in an incident with military forces in the Gulf of Oman, near the line the US Navy is using for its blockade of Iranian ports, and that investigations are ongoing. No vessel was named. The same day Iranian state media, quoting the Revolutionary Guard navy, said two tankers hit sea mines north of Ghanem Island, east of Hormuz. ABC News said it was not confirmed whether the reports describe the same event.

The incident follows days of talk in Tehran about breaking the blockade. Satellite images and tracking data show more than 14 tankers gathered around Kharg Island, Euronews reported, and Iran's deputy foreign minister called the blockade an act of war on Saturday, the day the US military marked 130 days of it. TankerTrackers said a tanker shown sailing through Hormuz on public tracking data was a spoof. On Monday Iran's foreign ministry spokesman said shipping could be restored quickly if the US lifts sanctions and the blockade and meets the terms of the June memorandum. Bahrain said an Iranian attack on Saturday started a fire at an industrial site in Sitra.

Traders so far are treating the reports as noise. OilPrice.com showed WTI at $83.15 and Brent at $91.98 shortly before 5 a.m. Central on Monday, both slightly lower on the day. The strait normally carries about a quarter of world seaborne oil trade, according to Euronews, and OPEC+ ministers meet on November 29 to set the 2027 framework.

Sources: Euronews; ABC News; Euronews; Fox News; OilPrice.com

Along the chain

  1. Upstream

    Gulf producers outside Iran still face attacks on energy sites; Bahrain's Sitra fire on Saturday is the latest.

  2. Midstream

    Tankers and insurers carry the risk. A convoy test or a confirmed interdiction would reprice freight and war risk cover east of Hormuz.

  3. Downstream

    Refiners that rely on Gulf crude and products watch for any disruption to loadings outside Iran, not just Iranian barrels.

  4. Market

    Brent near $92 and a Brent to WTI spread under $9 show little premium for the incident so far.

Use it

Producers
Treat the calm in futures as conditional. A confirmed seizure or a convoy attempt could move prices fast before the November 29 OPEC+ meeting.

Check war risk terms and routing for any voyage east of Hormuz this week.
Refiners
Review supply plans for Gulf grades and keep options open on Atlantic basin barrels.
Traders
Watch UKMTO notices and US Central Command statements; so far the market is pricing unconfirmed claims at close to zero.

Across the chain

Upstream

OffshoreNorway and the UK

Vår Energi agrees to buy ConocoPhillips' Norway business for $4.425 billion

ConocoPhillips said on November 23 it will sell its Norway business and its 40.25% stake in, and operatorship of, the Teesside oil terminal in England to Vår Energi for $4.425 billion, effective January 1, 2026. After adjustments and a 2026 tax bill of about $1.4 billion that Vår takes on, ConocoPhillips expects net cash of about $2.5 billion. The Norway business produced about 120,000 boe/d in 2025 and includes the operated Greater Ekofisk Area, eight partner operated fields and an interest in the Norpipe oil line. Closing is expected by the end of the first quarter of 2027, subject to approval by Norway's energy ministry.

Why it matters. Vår, majority owned by Eni, becomes operator of the Ekofisk hub and its export route to the UK. ConocoPhillips said the proceeds will go partly to share buybacks in 2027.

Sources: ConocoPhillips release; Offshore Energy; Investing.com, Reuters based

Regulation

GlobalAntalya, Türkiye

COP31 ends without an agreed deal after a fight over fossil fuel language

The UN climate talks in Antalya closed on Sunday, November 22, two days late, without a text adopted by all parties, the first such failure since Copenhagen in 2009, Al Jazeera reported. An opening draft called for a faster move away from fossil fuels; a later draft dropped every mention after Saudi Arabia and other oil and gas producers reportedly objected. The European Union said it would not back a deal that leaves fossil fuels out, and Panama formally objected.

Why it matters. Without a new text, the last agreed language on transitioning away from oil, gas and coal stands. Open questions included carbon capture and whether gas counts in power expansion.

Sources: Al Jazeera, with AP and Reuters

Midstream

OnshoreDelaware basin, New Mexico

San Mateo Midstream upsizes its notes to $850 million at 7.125%

San Mateo Midstream, owned 51% by Matador Resources and 49% by an affiliate of Five Point Infrastructure, priced $850 million of senior unsecured notes due 2031 at par on November 16, up from a planned $750 million. The money repays a $650 million term loan due July 2027 and part of its revolver. San Mateo gathers and processes gas, gathers oil and handles produced water in the northern Delaware basin.

Why it matters. Demand for the larger deal shows investors are still lending to Permian gathering and water systems, the same kind of asset Diamondback and KKR are buying in Deep Blue.

Sources: San Mateo release via Barchart; Baker Botts

Gas and LNG

Export terminalsUS Gulf Coast

US LNG feedgas eases from a record as Sabine Pass flows dip

Gas flows to US LNG plants fell to about 19.2 Bcf/d on November 17 from 19.5 Bcf/d the day before, Pipeline & Gas Journal reported from LSEG data. Sabine Pass dropped to about 4.2 Bcf/d from more than 5 Bcf/d, suggesting a train was down or running slow, and Freeport stayed near 1.5 Bcf/d against a usual 2 Bcf/d. Bloomberg had put flows at a record of about 21.8 Bcf/d on November 14.

Why it matters. Feedgas is now the biggest swing in daily US gas demand. Henry Hub futures were down about 8% early Monday on OilPrice.com.

Sources: Pipeline & Gas Journal, citing LSEG; Bloomberg via Energy Connects; OilPrice.com

Leader of the week · Week of November 23, 2026

NW

Nick Walker

Chief executive, Vår Energi

Walker's company agreed on November 23 to buy ConocoPhillips' Norway business and its Teesside terminal stake for an enterprise value of $4.425 billion, taking over operatorship of the Greater Ekofisk Area and the Norpipe export system. He has led Vår Energi, Norway's Eni controlled independent, since September 2023.

Before Vår he was chief executive of Lundin Energy until its sale to Aker BP in mid 2022, and he earlier worked at BP, Talisman Energy, Africa Oil and Cairn Oil & Gas under Vedanta. Vår says he has more than 30 years in technical, commercial and executive roles. He studied mining engineering at Imperial College London and computer science at University College London, and holds an MBA from City University Business School.

Why we are watching. The deal, on top of the BlueNord combination, puts Vår among the largest independent offshore producers, with a target of more than 500,000 boe/d into the mid 2030s. How he runs the ageing Ekofisk hub and its UK link will shape a large share of Norway's southern North Sea output.

Sources: Vår Energi executive committee; Offshore Engineer; ConocoPhillips release; Offshore Energy
