Midstream: Bakken outage

Bakken producers truck crude to Dakota Access as Enbridge's North Dakota line stays curtailed

A week into reduced flows on a 475,000 b/d pipeline, some barrels are moving by road, at least two Midwest refiners have declared force majeure and Enbridge has given no restart date.

Enbridge's North Dakota Pipeline has run at lower rates since last week, after reports of tainted crude led the company to test for organic chlorides, Bloomberg reported on Friday. Enbridge has not given a timeline for restoring normal flows on the 475,000 b/d system, Reuters reported earlier in the week.

Some producers and marketers are now trucking barrels to delivery points on the Dakota Access Pipeline, according to Energy Aspects analyst Jeremy Irwin, who said at least two Midwest refiners have declared force majeure on Bakken deliveries. A Reuters source named Flint Hills Resources' Pine Bend refinery in Minnesota as one, and Marathon Petroleum's MPLX unit declared force majeure on North Dakota deliveries on November 18. Energy Aspects expects the loss to tighten Midcontinent light sweet supply.

Bloomberg said the disruption could draw down crude stocks at Cushing, Oklahoma. The wider market is soft: December WTI settled at $83.98 on Friday, up 77 cents but down about 5% on the week, its third weekly drop, as tanker traffic through Hormuz returned to near prewar levels, Reuters reported.

Sources: Bloomberg via Insurance Journal; Reuters via BOE Report; Seeking Alpha; Reuters via Investing.com

Along the chain

  1. Upstream

    Bakken wells keep producing, but barrels that relied on Enbridge's line now need trucks or another pipe, which adds cost and squeezes wellhead netbacks.

  2. Midstream

    Dakota Access and truck terminals pick up volume. The organic chloride testing is the gate, and Enbridge has not said when it will finish.

  3. Downstream

    Refiners that run Bakken light sweet, including Pine Bend, have declared force majeure and must find other crude and avoid contaminated cargoes.

  4. Market

    Lost Bakken flows tighten Midcontinent light sweet supply and could pull barrels from Cushing, even while benchmark crude falls on wider supply.

Use it

Producers
Line up truck capacity and Dakota Access space now, and check contracts for force majeure terms while flows stay reduced.
Midstream
Crude quality testing can shut a system as surely as a leak. Review chloride screening at injection points and how fast you can isolate a bad batch.
Refiners
Midwest plants that run Bakken should confirm replacement barrels and test incoming crude for organic chlorides, which can damage units.
Traders
Watch Cushing stocks and Midcontinent light sweet differentials for the outage's effect, and Enbridge's restart notice for the end of it.

Across the chain

Midstream

OnshorePermian, Texas

Diamondback and KKR buy out Five Point's stake in the Deep Blue water network

Diamondback Energy and KKR agreed to buy Five Point Infrastructure's 70% stake in Deep Blue Midland Basin, the water joint venture Diamondback formed with Five Point in 2023. Neither side disclosed terms; Reuters, citing a source, put the price at about $2.1 billion. The network handles nearly 2 million b/d of water, and the deal is expected to close in the fourth quarter.

Why it matters. Produced water is now a core Permian midstream asset. An operator taking back control of its water system shows how much disposal and reuse capacity matters to drilling plans.

Sources: MarketScale, citing Reuters

Downstream

GlobalUnited States, China, Russia

US, China and Russia pledge more refined fuel supply but give no details

Presidents Trump, Xi and Putin met for about an hour and a half in Shenzhen on the sidelines of APEC on November 19. Their joint statement said the three countries plan to increase the production and supply of refined petroleum products, without saying how, Bloomberg reported. The diesel premium over crude on ICE Futures Europe fell as much as $2.84 to $58.55 a barrel. One analyst quoted by Al Jazeera called the statement political noise.

Why it matters. Diesel is the tightest part of the barrel after strikes on Russian refineries. Any real increase would most likely come from Chinese export quotas, which Beijing sets.

Sources: Bloomberg via Rigzone; Al Jazeera

Upstream

OnshoreUnited States

US rig count rises to 607, the highest since April 2024

Drillers added six oil rigs in the week to November 20, lifting the oil count to 469, its highest since May 2025, Baker Hughes data showed, Reuters reported. Gas rigs rose one to 128 and the total reached 607, up 53 from a year earlier.

Why it matters. Activity is rising even as crude posts a third weekly loss, a sign operators are still responding to the higher prices of earlier this year.

Sources: Reuters via BOE Report

Upstream

OnshorePermian, Texas

Presidio enters the Permian with a $360 million Midland basin deal

Presidio Production agreed on November 19 to buy two Rushmore Resource Partners companies for $360 million, adding 358 producing Spraberry and Wolfcamp wells in the southern Midland basin making about 12,800 boe/d, 46% of it gas. Presidio expects 2027 output to rise about 50%. The deal should close in the first quarter of 2027.

Why it matters. Buyers of low decline producing wells are now paying up in the Permian, not just in older basins. The gas and NGL heavy mix ties value to Waha and Gulf Coast pricing.

Sources: World Oil

Cross-industry

HydrogenChina

China puts its first pure hydrogen pipeline into service

A 195 km pipeline carrying green hydrogen from Darhan Muminggan Banner to users in Baotou, Inner Mongolia, has entered service, according to Xinhua as reported on November 20. It is designed for up to 100,000 tonnes a year but starts at about 7,800 tonnes, and the developer projects transport costs falling from about $1.94 to $0.45 per kg.

Why it matters. Dedicated hydrogen pipe is the missing link for large scale supply. Pipeline builders and steel makers elsewhere will watch how the line performs at higher volumes.

Sources: Interesting Engineering, citing Xinhua

Leader of the week · Week of November 16, 2026

DA

Dan Ammann

President, ExxonMobil Upstream Company

This month Ammann's upstream business took a $12.5 billion final investment decision on Longtail, a Stabroek development that targets non-associated gas and condensate rather than oil. He has run ExxonMobil's upstream business since February 1, 2025, after leading its Low Carbon Solutions unit from 2022.

Before ExxonMobil he was chief executive of Cruise, the GM majority owned self driving company, from 2018, and president of General Motors from 2014. When he moved to upstream he argued that meeting energy demand is an "and" equation: more supply and lower emissions together.

Why we are watching. An executive from autos and technology now runs one of the most closely followed growth assets in oil. Longtail tests whether Guyana can turn gas into a second engine, and his calls on pace and cost there will shape Stabroek's next decade.

Sources: ExxonMobil investor release; Oil & Gas Journal; Industrial Info
