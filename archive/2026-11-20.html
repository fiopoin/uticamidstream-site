LNG: federal approval

FERC approves Cheniere's three train Sabine Pass expansion and two new feed pipelines

The approval covers about 20 million tonnes a year of new liquefaction in Louisiana plus the Creole Trail and Sabine Crossing pipelines. Cheniere now has a clear path to a Phase 1 investment decision it expects in the coming months.

The Federal Energy Regulatory Commission on Thursday approved Cheniere's application to expand Sabine Pass LNG in Louisiana by about 20 million metric tons a year across three new trains, Reuters reported. NGI put the expansion at 19 million tonnes. FERC also authorized the Creole Trail Pipeline Project and the Sabine Crossing Pipeline to build new interstate pipe to feed the terminal.

Cheniere called the order a critical milestone on the way to a final investment decision on Phase 1, which it says would add more than 6 million tonnes a year by the early 2030s. That first phase is already sold under long term contracts, the company has financing lined up for half of it and has spent about $500 million on early works. NGI reports Cheniere is aiming for the Phase 1 decision around year end, with the first train adding about 0.8 to 0.9 Bcf/d of feed gas demand and the full buildout 2.5 to 2.7 Bcf/d.

The approval lands in a weak tape. WTI's December contract settled at $83.21 on Thursday and Brent at $91.98, both little changed, while the S&P 500 fell 2% in an AI led selloff. US gas storage rose 20 Bcf in the week to November 13, leaving inventories about 2.1% above average, and Henry Hub futures traded near $3.27 early Friday.

Sources: Reuters via Investing.com; Natural Gas Intelligence; LNG Prime; DTN; Natural Gas Intelligence

Along the chain

  1. Upstream

    A full buildout could need roughly 2.5 to 2.7 Bcf/d more feed gas, per NGI, a long dated source of demand for Gulf Coast gas supply.

  2. Midstream

    Creole Trail and Sabine Crossing are the new pipe into the terminal. Approval of the feed lines alongside the plant removes a common gap between terminal and supply permits.

  3. Downstream

    Phase 1 volumes are already sold under long term contracts, so the buyers are in place before the first steel goes in.

  4. Market

    More US export capacity in the early 2030s ties Henry Hub closer to global gas prices. The signal to watch is the timing of Cheniere's Phase 1 decision.

Use it

Producers
Gas focused operators near the Gulf Coast can treat Sabine Pass Phase 1 as likely new demand later this decade and plan supply deals around it.
Midstream
Feed pipelines approved with the terminal show how to package LNG and pipe applications. Watch for construction bids on Creole Trail and Sabine Crossing.
LNG buyers
Phase 1 is sold, but later trains are not. Buyers seeking non Gulf supply should expect Cheniere to market the remaining capacity.
Investors
The remaining step is the investment decision. Cost, financing terms and contract coverage for the later phases will set the value of the approval.

Across the chain

Midstream

OnshoreUnited States

Energy Transfer closes its $1.95 billion Vaquero Midstream purchase in the Delaware basin

Energy Transfer said on November 19 it completed its acquisition of Vaquero Midstream for $1.95 billion in cash plus about 33.3 million newly issued common units. The assets include the Caymus Processing Complex, with three processing trains and about 675 MMcf/d of capacity, and about 300 miles of pipeline in the Delaware basin.

Why it matters. Gas processing in the Permian keeps changing hands even as crude weakens. Owning plants near the wellhead gives Energy Transfer more gas and NGL volumes for its long haul pipes and export docks.

Sources: Energy Transfer

Cross-industry

GlobalMiddle East

IEA says Gulf bypass pipelines cannot end dependence on Hormuz

The IEA's World Energy Outlook, released November 19, estimates Gulf producers could spend about $40 billion on pipelines around the Strait of Hormuz. About 7 million b/d of new bypass capacity is under construction or being considered, adding about $2 a barrel, yet even in the IEA's most extreme diversification case about 2 million b/d would still pass the strait by 2035. Only the UAE's second Habshan to Fujairah line, adding 1.8 million b/d, is being built. Qatari and Emirati LNG has no feasible route around the strait.

Why it matters. Hormuz risk is structural, not temporary. That supports the case for supply from the Atlantic basin, including US LNG, even as the IEA warns of a buyers' market once the crisis passes.

Sources: The National

Midstream

OnshoreNorth Dakota

MPLX declares force majeure as Enbridge's Bakken line stays curtailed

Marathon Petroleum's midstream arm MPLX issued a force majeure on North Dakota pipeline deliveries after a run of crude contamination problems, Bloomberg reported late Wednesday. Flows on Enbridge's North Dakota Pipeline remained suppressed on Thursday morning, according to Wood Mackenzie, a sixth day of disruption. Enbridge is testing for organic chlorides, and the testing has caused the delays.

Why it matters. A second company has now invoked force majeure. The longer the line stays curtailed, the more Bakken barrels have to find room on Dakota Access or rail.

Sources: Seeking Alpha; Bloomberg via Bloomberg Tax

Downstream

RefiningRussia

Ukrainian strikes have cut Russian refinery runs by about 45%

Reuters reported on November 18 that Ukrainian strikes since late October have cut Russian crude processing by about 45%, from about 700,000 metric tons a day at the end of October to about 400,000. Attacks over 20 days disrupted or halted 15 of Russia's 35 major refineries, according to two industry sources. Reuters had put the cut at about 30% on November 10.

Why it matters. Less Russian refining means more crude for export and fewer products, especially diesel. That helps explain firm product cracks while crude futures sit near their lows.

Sources: Kyiv Independent, citing Reuters

Gas and LNG

OnshoreUnited States

US gas storage adds 20 Bcf, leaving stocks above average into winter

The EIA reported a 20 Bcf injection for the week ended November 13, NGI said, smaller than traders expected. Lower 48 inventories stood at 3,882 Bcf, about 2.1% above the five year average. Henry Hub futures were near $3.27 early Friday, down about 3%.

Why it matters. A bullish storage miss did not lift prices, which tells you the market is focused on mild weather and full storage. LNG feed gas demand is the main lever for tightening the balance.

Sources: Natural Gas Intelligence; OilPrice.com

Leader of the week · Week of November 16, 2026

DA

Dan Ammann

President, ExxonMobil Upstream Company

This month Ammann's upstream business took a $12.5 billion final investment decision on Longtail, a Stabroek development that targets non-associated gas and condensate rather than oil. He has run ExxonMobil's upstream business since February 1, 2025, after leading its Low Carbon Solutions unit from 2022.

Before ExxonMobil he was chief executive of Cruise, the GM majority owned self driving company, from 2018, and president of General Motors from 2014. When he moved to upstream he argued that meeting energy demand is an "and" equation: more supply and lower emissions together.

Why we are watching. An executive from autos and technology now runs one of the most closely followed growth assets in oil. Longtail tests whether Guyana can turn gas into a second engine, and his calls on pace and cost there will shape Stabroek's next decade.

Sources: ExxonMobil investor release; Oil & Gas Journal; Industrial Info
