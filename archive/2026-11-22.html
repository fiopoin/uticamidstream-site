Midstream: data center gas

Ohio permits 2,150 MW of gas power for data centers as Rockies Express seeks bids for a new lateral

The state siting board approved plants in Ashville and Millersport on November 19, and REX is taking binding bids to move up to 175,000 Dth/d to a site near Ashville.

The Ohio Power Siting Board on November 19 approved permits for two gas-fired plants that will power data center campuses in central Ohio, Signal Ohio reported. PowerConneX won approval for an 800 MW plant in Ashville to serve two data center buildings owned by its parent, EdgeConneX. Advanced Power won approval for the 1,350 MW Fairfield Energy Center in Millersport, which will feed a $2.1 billion Vantage Data Centers campus.

Both plants drew wide opposition at public hearings. In Ashville, more than 80% of voters backed repealing the village's emergency resolution on its development agreement with EdgeConneX, but the company told regulators the vote does not affect the plant, and the vote's effect after the board's ruling is unclear. Some projects still need separate environmental permits for water discharges or backup diesel generators.

Gas supply is the next step. Rockies Express Pipeline is seeking binding bids for a short lateral that would deliver up to 175,000 Dth/d to a new customer near Ashville that is building a behind-the-meter plant for a data center, NGI reported on November 20. Bids close Tuesday and service could start January 1, 2027. NGI's data show the REX Zone 3 delivered premium to Cheyenne narrowing from nearly $2/MMBtu in July to near zero this month.

Sources: Signal Ohio; NGI; Signal Ohio

Along the chain

  1. Upstream

    Producers that sell into Ohio gain a new class of buyer: plants built to serve a single data center campus rather than the grid.

  2. Midstream

    Short laterals off large interstate lines become the product. REX's binding bids close Tuesday, with service possible from January 1, 2027.

  3. Downstream

    The plants have siting permits but some still need water and air permits, and local opposition and a referendum in Ashville remain in the background.

  4. Market

    The REX Zone 3 premium to Cheyenne is near zero now, per NGI. New behind-the-meter load is the demand to watch against that.

Use it

Producers
Ask whether laterals to data center sites open firm sales you can contract directly, and on what term.
Midstream
Laterals to data center sites are a near term growth line. Treat local referendums and permit fights as schedule risk.
Power developers
Behind-the-meter gas is clearing Ohio's siting board, but expect hearings to focus on turbines, diesel backups and water.
Traders
Watch REX's bid results and the Zone 3 basis for signs that data center load is tightening Ohio supply.

Across the chain

Downstream

RefiningMinnesota and North Dakota

CVR Energy prices $1.25 billion of notes to fund its Marathon refinery purchase

CVR Energy priced $1.25 billion of 9.25% senior secured notes due 2032 at 98.98% of face on November 19, with closing expected November 25. The money, along with a planned $1.0 billion term loan and a larger credit line, will help fund its purchase of the St. Paul Park, Minnesota, and Mandan, North Dakota, refineries from Marathon Petroleum and MPLX. Until that deal closes, the proceeds sit in escrow.

Why it matters. A 9.25% coupon shows what lenders charge a midsize refiner for a large acquisition. Mandan sits in North Dakota, where Bakken crude is short on pipeline space during Enbridge's curtailment.

Sources: CVR Energy release via Business Wire; Oil Market Daily via PR Newswire

Midstream

ShippingIran, Persian Gulf

Iranian officials talk of running the US oil blockade as export revenue dries up

An Iranian lawmaker said on November 21 that 14 tankers were preparing to challenge the US naval blockade, though Tehran has not announced a plan. Kpler counts about 20 tankers near Kharg Island, most loaded and stationary, and projects Iran's crude and condensate export revenue will reach zero by mid-December. ABC News, citing UKMTO and IMO data, reported that attacks in and around Hormuz in October and November were the highest of any months since the war began.

Why it matters. A tanker convoy would test the blockade directly. Shipping insurers and Gulf exporters face higher risk even while overall Gulf flows recover.

Sources: RFE/RL via Eurasia Review; Arab Times; ABC News

Regulation

FederalUnited States

Senate passes a broad energy permitting bill, 62 to 33

The Senate passed a bipartisan bill to speed federal approval of large energy projects, including power plants, grid upgrades, data centers, oil, gas, coal, wind and solar, OilPrice.com reported citing Reuters. Fourteen Democrats and one independent joined Republicans. Critics said it weakens environmental review. The bill now goes to the House.

Why it matters. Faster permits would shorten timelines for pipelines and gas plants like the ones Ohio just approved, if the House agrees.

Sources: OilPrice.com, citing Reuters

Upstream

Onshore and offshoreNigeria

Nigeria opens registration for a 40 block licensing round

Nigeria's upstream regulator, NUPRC, opened the portal for its 2026 licensing round on November 20 and will take registrations until January 15, 2027. The 40 blocks on offer are 17 onshore, 14 in shallow water and nine in deep water. A pre-bid conference is set for Lagos on December 16.

Why it matters. The round tests whether Nigeria's 2021 petroleum law has made acreage attractive enough to pull in new operators.

Sources: BusinessDay

Downstream

Retail fuelUnited States

Thanksgiving gasoline could set a holiday price record despite a month long drop

The US average fell to $3.69 a gallon on November 20, nearly 60 cents below a month earlier, OilPrice.com reported. GasBuddy expects $3.62 on Thanksgiving Day, which would top the holiday record of $3.56 set in 2022. AAA expects 72.9 million people to travel by car. Diesel averaged about $5.90.

Why it matters. Pump prices are falling but are still about 60 cents above a year ago, and diesel remains far more expensive than gasoline.

Sources: OilPrice.com

Leader of the week · Week of November 16, 2026

DA

Dan Ammann

President, ExxonMobil Upstream Company

This month Ammann's upstream business took a $12.5 billion final investment decision on Longtail, a Stabroek development that targets non-associated gas and condensate rather than oil. He has run ExxonMobil's upstream business since February 1, 2025, after leading its Low Carbon Solutions unit from 2022.

Before ExxonMobil he was chief executive of Cruise, the GM majority owned self driving company, from 2018, and president of General Motors from 2014. When he moved to upstream he argued that meeting energy demand is an "and" equation: more supply and lower emissions together.

Why we are watching. An executive from autos and technology now runs one of the most closely followed growth assets in oil. Longtail tests whether Guyana can turn gas into a second engine, and his calls on pace and cost there will shape Stabroek's next decade.

Sources: ExxonMobil investor release; Oil & Gas Journal; Industrial Info
