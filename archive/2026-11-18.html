Crude and products

Crude slips toward $84 as a supply glut outruns the Hormuz war premium

Tankers are still hitting mines in the Gulf, yet oil keeps falling. Record US output, rising inventories and a rebound in Russian exports are winning the argument, and OPEC+ has to decide its 2027 plan in eleven days.

WTI traded at $84.42 and Brent at $92.13 on Wednesday morning, down more than 1% each. Brent was above $100 in early October, against about $73 before the Iran war began in late February.

The physical data point one way. The American Petroleum Institute reported a 5.3 million barrel crude build and a 5.1 million barrel gasoline build for the week to November 13, after a 9 million barrel crude build the week before. US crude output hit 14.013 million barrels a day in the week to November 6, a third straight weekly record, according to EIA figures. Russia loaded 4.32 million barrels a day of crude in the week to November 15, its highest since June, even as Ukraine struck 13 Russian refineries this month.

The risk has not gone away. Iran has reported near daily mine strikes on tankers in corridors it calls unauthorized, and LNG exits from Hormuz are running at about a third of prewar levels. The IEA expects a glut to appear this quarter and carry into next year. The seven core OPEC+ members kept December targets unchanged on November 1 and meet on November 29 to set the 2027 production framework.

Sources: OilPrice.com prices; OilPrice.com, API inventories; OilPrice.com, Russian exports; OilPrice.com, glut and Hormuz; Gulf News, OPEC+ October; Reuters via GV Wire, OPEC+ December

Along the chain

  1. Upstream

    Record US output at $84 WTI says operators are still drilling into this price. The test is 2027 budgets, which most producers set over the next two months.

  2. Midstream

    Cushing drew 1.69 million barrels even as national stocks built, so the hub is tighter than the headline. Barrels are leaving the Permian at a premium: WTI Midland futures last traded about $2 over WTI.

  3. Downstream

    Gasoline stocks sit 1% above the five year average while distillates are 8% below it. Diesel is still the margin, which keeps refiners running hard for middle distillates.

  4. Market

    The war premium is fading from futures faster than from physical cargoes in the Gulf. OPEC+ on November 29 is the next scheduled decision that can move the curve.

Across the chain

Midstream

OnshoreUnited States

Pipeline builders have proposed 46.5 Bcf/d of new US gas capacity this year

Platts counts 46.5 Bcf/d of proposed US gas pipeline projects announced in 2026, about 20 Bcf/d of it starting in Texas. The demand case rests on LNG terminals, power plants and data centers. Large Permian projects include the 4.5 Bcf/d Solitude system to Katy, which reached FID in August, and Kinder Morgan's 2.8 Bcf/d Permian Link, which reached FID on October 21. Energy Transfer is building laterals straight to data center campuses for Meta and Oracle. Most of this capacity is due between 2028 and 2030.

Why it matters. Takeaway sets the price producers get. More Permian egress should narrow Waha discounts by the end of the decade, and the data center laterals are a new kind of long term, utility style contract for pipeline owners.

Sources: S&P Global Energy

Upstream

OffshoreGuyana

ExxonMobil commits $12.5 billion to Longtail, a gas and condensate play in Guyana

ExxonMobil took a final investment decision on Longtail, the eighth development in the Stabroek block, on November 12. It targets non-associated gas and condensate in the southeast of the block, with about 45 subsea production and injection wells and first production planned for 2030. ExxonMobil operates with 45%, alongside Hess Guyana Exploration at 30% and CNOOC at 25%. The company produces about 900,000 barrels a day from four Stabroek projects, and expects to pass 1 million once Uaru starts.

Why it matters. Guyana is moving from an oil story to a gas story. Gas development opens questions about domestic power, an onshore gas pipeline and eventually LNG, all of which would need new infrastructure.

Sources: World Oil; Offshore Engineer; Industrial Info

Gas and LNG

GlobalEurope and the Gulf

European gas climbs back to 86 euros with storage far below normal

Dutch TTF front month gas rose 2% to 86 euros per MWh on Tuesday after a 7% drop on Monday. It hit a four year high above 100 euros in late October and was about 30 euros a year ago. EU storage is about 70% full against roughly 90% in prior years, and withdrawals have started. LNG exits from Hormuz are running at about a third of prewar levels, while gas flows to US LNG export plants hit a record over the weekend.

Why it matters. Henry Hub near $3.17 against TTF near 86 euros is one of the widest gaps on record, and it is the economic case for every US LNG project waiting on a permit or FID.

Sources: OilPrice.com

Downstream

RefiningUnited States

Refiners keep printing money as distillate stays scarce

US distillate inventories are 8% below the five year average while gasoline is 1% above, according to EIA data cited with this week's API report. Valero's third quarter net income rose to $6.8 billion from $1.1 billion a year earlier on stronger margins. The EIA has reported US refineries shifting output away from gasoline toward middle distillates and running at very high rates this year.

Why it matters. Product markets, not crude, are where 2026's supply shock has stuck. A refinery outage now moves margins more than a crude headline does.

Sources: OilPrice.com, API; OilPrice.com, Valero; EIA Today in Energy

Cross-industry

CCUSUnited States

Colorado becomes the seventh state to run its own CO2 storage permits

The Colorado Energy and Carbon Management Commission became the primary permitting and enforcement authority for Class VI CO2 storage wells on November 12, under an EPA rule published October 13. Colorado joins Arizona, Texas, Louisiana, West Virginia, Wyoming and North Dakota. In Texas, the Railroad Commission approved three Class VI wells in September for ExxonMobil's Rose project, designed to store about 53 million tonnes over 13 years.

Why it matters. State primacy has been the bottleneck breaker for CCS. For midstream companies, CO2 gathering and pipelines into permitted storage are becoming a real fee based business in oil states.

Sources: Greenberg Traurig; KUNC; Oil & Gas Journal

Leader of the week · Week of November 16, 2026

DA

Dan Ammann

President, ExxonMobil Upstream Company

This month Ammann's upstream business took a $12.5 billion final investment decision on Longtail, a Stabroek development that targets non-associated gas and condensate rather than oil. He has run ExxonMobil's upstream business since February 1, 2025, after leading its Low Carbon Solutions unit from 2022.

Before ExxonMobil he was chief executive of Cruise, the GM majority owned self driving company, from 2018, and president of General Motors from 2014. When he moved to upstream he argued that meeting energy demand is an "and" equation: more supply and lower emissions together.

Why we are watching. An executive from autos and technology now runs one of the most closely followed growth assets in oil. Longtail tests whether Guyana can turn gas into a second engine, and his calls on pace and cost there will shape Stabroek's next decade.

Sources: ExxonMobil investor release; Oil & Gas Journal; Industrial Info
