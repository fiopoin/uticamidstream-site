Downstream: Gulf refinery risk

Brent jumps 3.5% as the Houthis claim a strike on Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura refinery

Saudi air defenses said they downed four drones over the 550,000 b/d plant on Wednesday. Damage is unconfirmed, but satellite images showed thick smoke.

Yahya Saree, the Houthis' military spokesman, said on Wednesday that the group struck Ras Tanura, Saudi Arabia's largest refinery, and caused fires. Coalition spokesman Turki Al-Maliki said air defenses destroyed four drones over the site that morning and that fragments fell, with no injuries in an initial assessment, OilPrice.com reported. The coalition did not say the refinery was hit. Aramco and the Saudi government did not respond to Reuters, and NASA imagery reviewed by Reuters showed thick black smoke over about 600 square kilometers.

The claim came a day after a strike on the Riyadh tower that houses the Public Investment Fund wounded 11 people, and after coalition strikes on Yemen's Hodeidah port, The National reported. On Thursday the coalition said it intercepted two ballistic missiles fired at Riyadh, two drones over the Eastern Province and a cruise missile aimed at the west of the kingdom. Ras Tanura handles about 16% of Saudi refining capacity, and its terminal can load about 6.5 million b/d.

Brent settled up $3.21, or 3.5%, at $94.61 and WTI rose $1.63 to $83.68, Reuters reported. Tamas Varga of PVM said traders bid up crude to cover disruption risk and bought back short positions before the holiday. Tsuyoshi Ueno of NLI Research Institute said the prospect of surplus supply capped gains. EIA data showed US commercial crude stocks up 1.1 million barrels to 418.9 million in the week to November 20.

Sources: Reuters via The Nation Thailand; OilPrice.com; The National; OilPrice.com

Along the chain

  1. Upstream

    A $3 risk premium came back in one session. Producers get a better hedge window, but OPEC+ sets 2027 policy on Sunday.

  2. Midstream

    The Ras Tanura terminal can load about 6.5 million b/d, so any hit to the export side would matter far more than the refinery itself.

  3. Downstream

    Gasoline and diesel futures fell while crude rose, and the 3-2-1 crack narrowed to about $35.36 from about $38.70 a day earlier.

  4. Market

    Brent's premium to WTI widened to about $10.62 from about $9.43 a day earlier, since the risk sits in the Gulf, not the US.

Across the chain

Upstream

Global11 countries outside Russia

US-led consortium agrees to buy Lukoil's international business

A group led by US asset manager Eldridge agreed on November 25 to acquire Lukoil International, which holds the Russian company's oil and gas fields, refineries, pipelines and other assets in 11 countries, AP reported. The consortium includes the US International Development Finance Corp., Catena Investment Holding of the UAE and Xtellus Capital Partners. Production is about 350,000 boe/d, the group said. No price was given. Instead of paying cash to Russia, the buyers would swap restricted Russian shares of Lukoil and other Russian companies for Lukoil International's liquidity. Treasury had set a February 28 deadline for Lukoil to sell its foreign assets.

Why it matters. It would put a large foreign upstream and refining portfolio under US backed ownership, with a federal lender holding a stake. Rep. Gregory Meeks objected that the DFC is meant for development in emerging markets.

Sources: AP via WSLS

Upstream

OPEC+Iraq

Iraq disputes the capacity number behind its 2027 OPEC+ quota

Outside consultants hired for OPEC+'s first independent capacity review put Iraq's maximum sustainable output below 5 million b/d, Forbes reported on November 25, citing Bloomberg. Oil Minister Basim Mohammed Khudair wrote to OPEC that Iraq can produce 5.2 million b/d. The IEA puts sustainable capacity at 4.87 million b/d. Iraq's December target is 4.431 million b/d. For October it told OPEC it produced 4.888 million b/d, while OPEC's secondary sources had 4.491 million b/d. Kazakhstan has also sought a higher estimate. Ministers meet online on November 29 to set 2027 levels, which would use the capacity figures as baselines.

Why it matters. Capacity baselines decide who gets to grow in 2027. A bigger Iraqi number means more barrels in a market already worried about surplus, if Iraq can export them.

Sources: Forbes

Gas and LNG

OnshoreUnited States

Winter's first storage draw comes in at 24 Bcf

EIA reported a 24 Bcf net withdrawal from Lower 48 storage for the week ended November 20, the first of the heating season, NGI said. The draw matched the five year average for the week and landed near consensus. The East led the declines, and NGI called the weather outlook largely bearish. NYMEX gas was quoted at $3.682/MMBtu in late Wednesday trade on OilPrice.com, up less than 1% after Tuesday's rally of about 5%.

Why it matters. A normal first draw with record supply and a mild outlook leaves little cushion for bulls. Cold has to show up for prices to hold near $3.70.

Sources: NGI; OilPrice.com

Downstream

RefiningUnited States

US refiners run at 97.5% as fuel stocks build

Refinery inputs rose 428,000 b/d to 17.3 million b/d in the week ended November 20, and utilization reached 97.5% of capacity, EIA data cited by Oil & Gas Journal show. Gasoline stocks rose 1.7 million barrels and sit 3% above the five year average. Distillate stocks rose 700,000 barrels but are still 6% below average. Commercial crude stocks rose 1.1 million barrels to 418.9 million, helped by imports up 673,000 b/d to 6.2 million b/d.

Why it matters. Plants are running near flat out into winter. With gasoline already ample, diesel is the product that keeps margins up, and Wednesday's fall in fuel futures shows how fast that can turn.

Sources: Oil & Gas Journal

Regulation

OnshoreTexas

Texas Railroad Commission investigates Atmos Energy's pipe safety program

The Railroad Commission of Texas is investigating Atmos Energy, the Dallas gas utility with more than 2 million Texas customers, over seven alleged violations found in a routine review of its distribution integrity management program, the Texas Tribune reported November 25. Fines could reach $2 million. The commission cited missing records, flawed risk scoring for plastic pipe and no clear rules for tracking equipment failures, and said Atmos could not show it replaced at least 8% of its riskiest pipe. Revised risk and reporting plans are due December 2. Atmos disputes parts of the findings.

Why it matters. It is a test of how hard the commission will push on gas distribution safety as Jon Rosenthal, the first Democrat to win a seat in 36 years, prepares to join it.

Sources: Texas Tribune; Bloomberg via Rigzone

Gas and LNG

OffshoreArgentina

Argentina LNG clears the RIGI incentive regime at $50.75 billion

Argentina's government approved the Argentina LNG project for its RIGI large investment regime, covering US$50.75 billion through 2050, DataPortuaria reported on November 21. YPF holds 36%, with Eni and ADNOC's XRG at 32% each. The plan uses two floating liquefaction units off Río Negro, each with 6 million tonnes a year of capacity, fed by Vaca Muerta gas. About US$25 billion would be spent from 2027 to 2030, and the US Export Import Bank has proposed up to US$6 billion. DataPortuaria said the final investment decision is set for November 30.

Why it matters. It would be the first large LNG export project from Vaca Muerta and a new competitor for US Gulf Coast cargoes after 2030.

Sources: DataPortuaria

Leader of the week · Week of November 23, 2026

NW

Nick Walker

Chief executive, Vår Energi

Walker's company agreed on November 23 to buy ConocoPhillips' Norway business and its Teesside terminal stake for an enterprise value of $4.425 billion, taking over operatorship of the Greater Ekofisk Area and the Norpipe export system. He has led Vår Energi, Norway's Eni controlled independent, since September 2023.

Before Vår he was chief executive of Lundin Energy until its sale to Aker BP in mid 2022, and he earlier worked at BP, Talisman Energy, Africa Oil and Cairn Oil & Gas under Vedanta. Vår says he has more than 30 years in technical, commercial and executive roles. He studied mining engineering at Imperial College London and computer science at University College London, and holds an MBA from City University Business School.

Why we are watching. The deal, on top of the BlueNord combination, puts Vår among the largest independent offshore producers, with a target of more than 500,000 boe/d into the mid 2030s. How he runs the ageing Ekofisk hub and its UK link will shape a large share of Norway's southern North Sea output.

Sources: Vår Energi executive committee; Offshore Engineer; ConocoPhillips release; Offshore Energy
