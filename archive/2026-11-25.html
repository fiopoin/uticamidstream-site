Market: shipping risk versus surplus

Oil gives back Tuesday's gain as glut fears outweigh new ship attacks near Hormuz

Brent fell back to about $90 early Wednesday, a day after rising more than 2% when three vessels were struck by projectiles in the strait.

Crude swung both ways in 24 hours. On Tuesday, Brent settled up 2.27% at $91.40 and WTI up 2.35% at $82.05, Forbes Middle East reported, on shipping risk in the Strait of Hormuz. By 07:10 Saudi time on Wednesday, Reuters reported, both had lost $1.35, with Brent at $90.05 and WTI at $80.70.

The attacks continued. The UK Maritime Trade Operations agency said three vessels were struck by unidentified projectiles on Monday while sailing east through the strait, and Iran's Revolutionary Guards said they stopped a container ship on an "unauthorised" route on Tuesday, according to Reuters. ANZ analysts said flows through Hormuz were at 49% of normal levels, Angel One reported.

Supply still sets the tone. Zhenqiang Seto of LSEG told Reuters that surplus worries for 2027 outweigh shipping risk. Industry data from the American Petroleum Institute showed US crude stocks up 126,000 barrels in the week to November 20, against an average forecast of a 1.5 million barrel rise in a Reuters poll. Iraq is pressing OPEC+ to raise its estimate of Iraqi production capacity, four people familiar with the matter told Reuters, ahead of the group's November 29 meeting on 2027 policy.

Sources: Reuters via Arab News; Forbes Middle East; Angel One; OilPrice.com

Along the chain

  1. Upstream

  2. Midstream

    Tankers keep taking hits east of Hormuz and flows are about half of normal, so war risk cover and routing costs stay high.

  3. Downstream

    Diesel futures were flat on Tuesday while crude rose, and the 3-2-1 crack eased to about $38.70.

  4. Market

    Each incident lifts prices for a session, then the surplus view returns. The Brent to WTI spread widened to about $9.43.

Across the chain

Upstream

OnshorePennsylvania, United States

POSCO closes its $550 million Marcellus buy from Chord Energy

POSCO International said it finalized the purchase of Chord Energy's entire stake in a Pennsylvania shale gas field on November 23, with a signing ceremony in Houston, Korea JoongAng Daily reported. When the deal was announced in September, Oil & Gas Journal said the non-operated position covers about 32,000 net acres in the core of the Marcellus and produced about 121 MMcf/d over the trailing twelve months, with 1.3 Tcf of reserves. The local operator keeps running the wells; POSCO handles marketing. POSCO expects about 100 billion won, about $74 million, of operating profit a year.

Why it matters. Asian buyers are moving from buying US LNG to owning the gas behind it. POSCO plans to send part of the output to LNG plants from 2029, OGJ said, while Chord narrows to the Williston Basin.

Sources: Korea JoongAng Daily; Oil & Gas Journal

Downstream

RefiningNigeria

Dangote refinery heads for a stock listing worth about N65 trillion

Nigeria's Securities and Exchange Commission approved the Dangote refinery's share allotment plan on November 23, the last regulatory step before listing, BusinessDay reported. Brokers expect the shares to debut as early as December 1, though revised timetables had pointed to December 8. The IPO sold 4.1 billion shares at N525, about 3.3% of the company, and drew about N5.85 trillion of applications. At the offer price the refinery is valued near N65.2 trillion. Capacity was rerated to 700,000 b/d in July, with a plan for 1.4 million b/d by 2029.

Why it matters. It would be the largest listed company in Africa, and the proceeds help fund a $14.3 billion expansion that would reshape fuel trade flows into West Africa, long a market for US and European gasoline.

Sources: BusinessDay

Gas and LNG

Export terminalsSouth Texas

Rio Grande LNG asks to bring fuel gas into its first train

A NextDecade affiliate has filed to introduce high pressure fuel gas into the Rio Grande LNG plant under construction near Brownsville, NGI reported on November 24, a step that brings the first liquefaction train closer to start up. First LNG remains on track for the second quarter of 2027. NextDecade has started pre-filing for two more trains, and NGI said the site could eventually reach 48 million tonnes a year.

Why it matters. Commissioning gas is the first new feedgas pull from a Texas project in this cycle, adding demand on South Texas pipelines well before the first cargo.

Sources: NGI

Gas and LNG

OnshoreUnited States

First storage draw of the winter is due in today's early EIA report

NGI expects the EIA report for the week ended November 20 to show a 25 Bcf withdrawal, the first of the 2026 to 2027 heating season and in line with the five year average. The report comes at noon Eastern on Wednesday, a day early because of Thanksgiving. NGI said Lower 48 dry gas output rose 3.0 Bcf/d to 111.1 Bcf/d last week, LNG feedgas was about 20.7 Bcf/d, and Baker Hughes and Primary Vision showed the biggest combined jump in rigs and frac spreads since October 9.

Why it matters. Supply is climbing just as heating demand arrives. A smaller draw than expected would test Tuesday's rally of about 5% in NYMEX gas futures.

Sources: NGI; OilPrice.com

Upstream

OnshoreMidland Basin, Texas

Diversified closes its $1.8 billion Birch Permian deal

Diversified Energy completed its purchase of Birch Permian Holdings on November 19 for about $1.8 billion, its 8-K shows. The deal brings about 46,000 net acres in the Midland Basin, 480 net operated wells, and midstream and water infrastructure. To help pay, a subsidiary sold $1.455 billion of fixed rate asset backed notes due 2046, secured by Midland wellbore interests, with coupons from 6.68% to 10.756%.

Why it matters. Securitizing producing wells is spreading from Appalachia to the Permian, giving buyers of mature acreage a funding route outside bank loans.

Sources: Diversified Energy 8-K

Cross-industry

Power and data centersUnited States

Data centers plan more than 115 GW of their own gas plants

Data center developers had announced more than 100 behind the meter gas power plants in the US as of October, with planned capacity above 115 GW, MIT Technology Review reported on November 24, citing Cleanview data. About 4.9 GW is running and 15.8 GW is under construction. BloombergNEF counts 181 GW of announced on-site data center gas projects worldwide, 90% of it in the US. "The biggest motivator for on-site power right now from data centers is speed," said Musfika Mishi of BloombergNEF.

Why it matters. That is new gas demand that bypasses the grid queue but not the pipeline, so laterals, compression and supply contracts near data center sites become the bottleneck.

Sources: MIT Technology Review

Leader of the week · Week of November 23, 2026

NW

Nick Walker

Chief executive, Vår Energi

Walker's company agreed on November 23 to buy ConocoPhillips' Norway business and its Teesside terminal stake for an enterprise value of $4.425 billion, taking over operatorship of the Greater Ekofisk Area and the Norpipe export system. He has led Vår Energi, Norway's Eni controlled independent, since September 2023.

Before Vår he was chief executive of Lundin Energy until its sale to Aker BP in mid 2022, and he earlier worked at BP, Talisman Energy, Africa Oil and Cairn Oil & Gas under Vedanta. Vår says he has more than 30 years in technical, commercial and executive roles. He studied mining engineering at Imperial College London and computer science at University College London, and holds an MBA from City University Business School.

Why we are watching. The deal, on top of the BlueNord combination, puts Vår among the largest independent offshore producers, with a target of more than 500,000 boe/d into the mid 2030s. How he runs the ageing Ekofisk hub and its UK link will shape a large share of Norway's southern North Sea output.

Sources: Vår Energi executive committee; Offshore Engineer; ConocoPhillips release; Offshore Energy
