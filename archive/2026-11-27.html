Midstream: Gulf export risk

Brent settles at $96.57 as Iran reports mine strikes and Ras Tanura loadings reportedly halt

Iran's Revolutionary Guards said two tankers hit mines off Oman on Thursday, and CNN reported Saudi loadings stopped at the Gulf's biggest crude terminal.

Iran's Revolutionary Guards said on Thursday that two tankers exploded and caught fire after hitting sea mines north of Oman's Khasab port while crossing the Strait of Hormuz on what the Guards called an unauthorised route, Reuters reported. One was a supertanker carrying more than 2 million barrels of crude, according to a statement carried by Tasnim. The UK Maritime Trade Operations agency separately reported an explosion from an unknown projectile near a tanker about 50 nautical miles west of Dubai. Guards spokesman Hossein Mohebbi said the force was using heavy firepower to keep the strait closed.

On the Saudi side, CNN reported, citing sources, that oil loadings at Ras Tanura were halted after Wednesday's Houthi attack and that it was unclear when they would resume. Saudi Arabia and Aramco have not confirmed a halt. The Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted four drones over the area on Wednesday but did not confirm a hit on the refinery, which sits inside a complex that is also a major crude export terminal.

Brent rose $1.96, or 2.07%, to settle at $96.57 on Thursday, The Nation reported. There was no WTI settlement because of Thanksgiving; WTI traded at $84.84 late in the day, up $1.16. Rory Johnston of Commodity Context said Brent had been oversold below $90 and put about 15% of global supply under a known, acute risk. ANZ's Daniel Hynes told Reuters the wider conflict threatens the recent recovery in Gulf flows.

Sources: Reuters via Arab News; Reuters via Baird Maritime; Newsquawk, citing CNN; The Nation Thailand; Reuters via Yahoo Finance; Trading Economics

Across the chain

Midstream

OnshorePermian Basin, Texas

Targa is in talks to buy Blackstone backed Kinetik, Bloomberg reports

Targa Resources is in talks to acquire Kinetik Holdings, the Delaware Basin gas gatherer and processor backed by Blackstone, Bloomberg News reported on November 25, citing people familiar with the matter, according to Reuters. A deal could be sealed as soon as next week. Neither company responded to Reuters. Kinetik closed at $54.70 that day and rose about 7.6% after hours, while Targa closed at $285.53 and slipped about 1.8%, 24/7 Wall St. reported. No price or structure has been reported.

Why it matters. Reuters noted that gas demand from data centers has drawn heavy interest in energy infrastructure. A deal would consolidate Delaware Basin processing, leaving producers there with fewer counterparties.

Sources: Reuters via BOE Report; 24/7 Wall St.

Upstream

OPEC+Global

OPEC+ goes into Sunday expected to hold output steady into 2027

OPEC+ ministers meet online on Sunday, November 29, at 1pm GMT, the first of four ministerial meetings. Two people familiar with the talks expect output targets to stay unchanged at least through the first quarter of 2027, The Nation reported. Iraq objects to a preliminary capacity figure of 4.9 million b/d and Kazakhstan also wants a higher number. Russian Deputy Prime Minister Alexander Novak said on November 25 the capacity review would be done by year end. The audits were due by the end of September and slipped to mid November, The Private Banker reported.

Why it matters. A rollover keeps barrels off a market Rystad sees in surplus by about 5 million b/d in 2027, while the Gulf attacks argue for caution. The capacity baselines, not Sunday's headline, decide who grows later.

Sources: The Nation Thailand; The Private Banker

Gas and LNG

OffshoreSuriname

Petronas and Staatsolie take FID on Suriname's first offshore gas project

Petronas and state company Staatsolie made a final investment decision on KrakaWi, a $1.6 billion development of the Sloanea gas field in Block 52 offshore Suriname, Rigzone reported on November 27. The field holds an estimated 97 billion cubic meters, about 3.43 Tcf, of recoverable gas. A floating LNG unit would produce up to about 1.5 million tonnes a year for 25 years, with gas expected from mid 2030. Petronas operates with 80% and Staatsolie holds 20%. Staatsolie's Annand Jagesar said the gas could also feed domestic power later.

Why it matters. It opens a new gas province on the Guyana Suriname trend and adds a small Atlantic LNG supplier after 2030, alongside Argentina's planned exports.

Sources: Rigzone

Midstream

OnshoreBritish Columbia

First Nations get C$168 million loan guarantee for a Coastal GasLink stake

Twelve British Columbia First Nations in the CGL First Nations Limited Partnership received a loan guarantee of about C$168 million to invest in the entity that holds TC Energy's interest in Coastal GasLink, The Canadian Press reported on November 26. The pipeline carries gas from northeastern BC to LNG Canada at Kitimat. It is the fifth deal closed by the federal Canada Indigenous Loan Guarantee Corp. since 2024 and the second Indigenous investment tied to the line; five other Nations put in C$65.3 million in October. TC and Indigenous partners hold 35% together, with KKR and AIMCo owning the rest.

Why it matters. Indigenous equity is becoming a standard part of Canadian LNG infrastructure, and TC has approved an expansion of the line for LNG Canada's second phase.

Sources: The Canadian Press via CP24

Cross-industry

ShippingStrait of Hormuz

Iran's mine claims pile up as it ties reopening Hormuz to US demands

Iranian forces claimed on November 25 that an LNG carrier hit a naval mine and caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz, and Fars said an oil tanker hit a mine in a restricted zone, Reuters reported; Reuters could not independently verify the claims. Iranian army spokesman Mohammad Akraminia said new systems had been deployed to watch and control the strait. Tehran has told Washington normal shipping will not return until its demands are met, including lifting the US naval blockade of Iranian ports, and through Qatari mediators offered to reopen the waterway within 48 hours if they are.

Why it matters. Two days of mine claims plus Thursday's tanker fires make Gulf transits a daily insurance and routing decision for crude, LNG and product shippers.

Sources: Reuters via Arab News

Leader of the week · Week of November 23, 2026

NW

Nick Walker

Chief executive, Vår Energi

Walker's company agreed on November 23 to buy ConocoPhillips' Norway business and its Teesside terminal stake for an enterprise value of $4.425 billion, taking over operatorship of the Greater Ekofisk Area and the Norpipe export system. He has led Vår Energi, Norway's Eni controlled independent, since September 2023.

Before Vår he was chief executive of Lundin Energy until its sale to Aker BP in mid 2022, and he earlier worked at BP, Talisman Energy, Africa Oil and Cairn Oil & Gas under Vedanta. Vår says he has more than 30 years in technical, commercial and executive roles. He studied mining engineering at Imperial College London and computer science at University College London, and holds an MBA from City University Business School.

Why we are watching. The deal, on top of the BlueNord combination, puts Vår among the largest independent offshore producers, with a target of more than 500,000 boe/d into the mid 2030s. How he runs the ageing Ekofisk hub and its UK link will shape a large share of Norway's southern North Sea output.

Sources: Vår Energi executive committee; Offshore Engineer; ConocoPhillips release; Offshore Energy
