Upstream: OPEC's membership

US pushed Venezuela and Iraq to weigh leaving OPEC, Reuters reports, days before OPEC+ meets

Ten people told Reuters Washington encouraged both founding members to consider an exit this year. OPEC+ ministers meet Sunday expecting to hold output steady.

The Trump administration encouraged Venezuela and Iraq earlier this year to consider leaving OPEC and stopped Venezuela from paying its annual membership dues, Reuters reported on November 27, citing 10 people familiar with the matter. The State Department opened talks with Venezuelan officials after the US seized Maduro in January, five of the sources said. The US controls Venezuela's access to state funds, and Iraq's oil revenue passes through an account at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York.

In Iraq, US officials held talks with Prime Minister Ali Faleh al-Zaidi this summer, two sources said. He raised leaving OPEC at a government meeting, but the oil ministry persuaded him to stay and push for a higher quota instead. Two sources said Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman intervened this summer to stop the US pressing further on Venezuela. A State Department official said leaving OPEC had not been raised by high level officials and that talks with Iraq focused on energy infrastructure. OPEC, Venezuela, Iraq and Saudi Arabia did not comment.

The report lands two days before OPEC+ ministers meet on Sunday, November 29, to set policy for 2027. Three people familiar with the talks told Reuters the group, led by Saudi Arabia and Russia, is set to keep first quarter 2027 targets unchanged. Iraq and Kazakhstan want higher capacity figures recognised, QNC News reported. The UAE left OPEC earlier this year. Brent settled Friday at $95.44, up about 3% on the week.

Sources: Reuters via BNN Bloomberg; Reuters via AsiaOne; QNC News; OilPrice.com

Along the chain

  1. Upstream

    Iraq is pressing for a bigger quota, and Venezuela is exempt from OPEC+ cuts and aims for 1.5 million b/d. Either exit would add barrels outside the group's control.

  2. Midstream

    More Iraqi and Venezuelan crude would test export routes already strained by Gulf attacks and Hormuz risk.

  3. Downstream

    Refiners face a 3-2-1 crack near $37 on Friday's quotes, up from about $35.65 a day earlier, with diesel still about $53 a barrel over gasoline.

  4. Market

    Brent's premium to WTI is about $12 on Friday's quotes. A weaker OPEC would lean against that premium over time, while the Gulf attacks hold it up now.

Across the chain

Midstream

OnshoreAlberta and British Columbia

Trans Mountain finishes its flow boost early as quarterly shipments rise

Trans Mountain finished a project that uses drag reducing chemical additives to raise crude flow on its pipeline two months ahead of schedule, the Crown corporation said with third quarter results on November 27, The Canadian Press reported. Capacity rises up to about 10% to 980,000 barrels a day. Shipments averaged 840,000 b/d in the quarter, up from 777,000 a year earlier, and about 60% went to the Westridge Marine Terminal. Net income was C$132 million on revenue of C$825 million. It sent C$551 million to Ottawa in the quarter and C$1.45 billion so far this year.

Why it matters. More West Coast capacity gives Canadian heavy crude another route to Asia and California, which competes with Gulf barrels. A further 210,000 b/d expansion is expected by the end of 2028.

Sources: The Canadian Press via Global News

Downstream

RefiningJazan, Saudi Arabia

Projectile strike at Aramco's Jazan refinery kills a worker and halts some units

An aerial projectile hit Aramco's Jazan refinery on November 26, killing a Nepali worker, injuring several others and starting a fire that temporarily halted some operations, the Saudi energy ministry said, Al Jazeera and Reuters reported. The plant near the Yemeni border can process up to 400,000 barrels a day. The ministry did not name the attacker; the Saudi led coalition blamed the Houthis and later said it destroyed a ballistic missile launch platform in Hodeidah. The Houthis had claimed attacks on Ras Tanura and on the Red Sea ports of Jeddah and Jazan the day before.

Why it matters. It is the second Saudi refinery targeted in two days and the first on the Red Sea side, which is where Saudi exports would turn if Gulf terminals were disrupted.

Sources: Al Jazeera, with Reuters; Reuters via AsiaOne

Regulation

OnshoreUS Gulf Coast, Appalachia, Permian

FERC approves Sabine Pass expansion and four more gas projects

FERC voted unanimously to approve five natural gas projects, BIC Magazine reported on November 27. Cheniere's Sabine Pass in Cameron Parish, Louisiana gets three more liquefaction trains, along with an expanded Creole Trail Pipeline and a new Sabine Crossing pipeline. Mountain Valley's MVP Boost adds 600,000 Dth/d of firm capacity in Virginia and West Virginia. Targa's Forza Pipeline would run about 36 miles from Lea County, New Mexico to Waha with up to 750,000 Dth/d. Columbia Gas won a Virginia LNG storage plant and Texas Eastern a 125,000 Dth/d Mississippi expansion. Chairman Laura Swett said the commission is delivering on its commitment.

Why it matters. Forza gives Delaware Basin gas another path to Waha, where takeaway has been tight, and Sabine Pass adds to the next wave of US LNG supply.

Sources: BIC Magazine; Oklahoma Energy Today

Cross-industry

ShippingGulf of Oman and Strait of Hormuz

Brent posts first weekly gain of November as Gulf attacks continue

Brent rose about 3% this week, its first weekly gain in November after three losing weeks, though it fell 1.2% on Friday to $95.44, Reuters reported. Iran has kept attacking ships in the Strait of Hormuz, the Houthis struck Saudi infrastructure, and the US said it disabled a commercial ship that tried to run its blockade of Iran. An Iranian adviser said Iran would keep the strait shut. Ukraine has stepped up drone attacks on Russian refineries since late October. ANZ analysts said Brent can top $100 without a new escalation only if deliverable supply proves tight.

Why it matters. Supply risk is spread across three regions at once, which keeps a floor under Brent even as forecasters see a surplus next year.

Sources: Reuters via AsiaOne

Leader of the week · Week of November 23, 2026

NW

Nick Walker

Chief executive, Vår Energi

Walker's company agreed on November 23 to buy ConocoPhillips' Norway business and its Teesside terminal stake for an enterprise value of $4.425 billion, taking over operatorship of the Greater Ekofisk Area and the Norpipe export system. He has led Vår Energi, Norway's Eni controlled independent, since September 2023.

Before Vår he was chief executive of Lundin Energy until its sale to Aker BP in mid 2022, and he earlier worked at BP, Talisman Energy, Africa Oil and Cairn Oil & Gas under Vedanta. Vår says he has more than 30 years in technical, commercial and executive roles. He studied mining engineering at Imperial College London and computer science at University College London, and holds an MBA from City University Business School.

Why we are watching. The deal, on top of the BlueNord combination, puts Vår among the largest independent offshore producers, with a target of more than 500,000 boe/d into the mid 2030s. How he runs the ageing Ekofisk hub and its UK link will shape a large share of Norway's southern North Sea output.

Sources: Vår Energi executive committee; Offshore Engineer; ConocoPhillips release; Offshore Energy
